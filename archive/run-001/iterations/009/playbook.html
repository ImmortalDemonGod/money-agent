The 2026 Website Audit Playbook

The exact 42-point method I run on landing pages, in priority order. Work top to bottom; the red items move revenue first. Thank you for buying it. This page is yours to keep.

Every audit answers four questions in order: Can machines find and understand this page? (SEO + AI visibility), Is it fast enough that people stay? (performance), Does it make the visitor act? (conversion), and Will it break trust? (reliability). Each check below has a one-line fix. A page that passes the eleven red (P1) checks is already ahead of most of the web.

I. Findability — SEO machines

P1 · One descriptive <title>, 50–60 charsPrimary keyword + the benefit. It is the biggest single on-page ranking and click-through lever. Truncates past ~60 chars.
P1 · A meta description, 140–160 charsDoesn't rank you, but it is your SERP ad copy — write it to earn the click, end with a soft CTA.
P1 · Exactly one <h1>, stating the value propositionZero H1s or several confuse crawlers and skim-readers alike. One clear promise.
P1 · Not accidentally noindexCheck the robots meta and the X-Robots-Tag header. A stray noindex means you will never rank, no matter what else you do. The most expensive one-line bug on the web.
P2 · Canonical URL set<link rel="canonical"> stops duplicate-URL variants (trailing slash, UTM, http/https) from splitting your ranking signals.
P2 · Logical heading hierarchy (H1→H2→H3, no skips)Structure is how both screen readers and AI extractors understand your argument.
P2 · Descriptive alt text on meaningful imagesAccessibility + image search + AI extractability. Decorative images get empty alt="".
P3 · robots.txt with a Sitemap: line, and a real sitemapCheap insurance that every page gets discovered and recrawled.
P3 · lang attribute on <html>Small signal, trivial fix, helps i18n and accessibility.

II. AI-search visibility (GEO) — the 2026 layer machines

Answer engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, Claude) now sit between you and a large slice of your would-be traffic. They cite pages they can read as text and trust as structured. Most sites have done nothing here — which is exactly the opening.

P1 · schema.org JSON-LD present and validThe single biggest AI-visibility lever. Add Organization, Product, FAQPage, or Article as <script type="application/ld+json">. This is how machines know who you are and what to cite. Validate at validator.schema.org.
P1 · Core value copy is in the server HTML, not JS-onlyIf your headline and value prop are painted in by client JS, many AI crawlers never see them. View source (not the rendered DOM): is your pitch actually in the bytes?
P2 · A /llms.txt fileThe emerging convention telling AI crawlers what your site is and what to cite. A first-mover edge in 2026; costs ten minutes.
P2 · Answer-shaped content (question headings, direct answers)Phrase an H2 as the question a user asks, then answer it in the first sentence. Answer engines lift these verbatim.
P2 · A real FAQ with FAQPage schemaDoubles as SERP rich results and as pre-chewed citable Q&A for AI.
P3 · Don't block AI crawlers you want citations fromDecide deliberately on GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended in robots.txt — blocking them forfeits the citation.

III. Speed — performance people

P1 · Load under ~2.5s; largest content paints fastBounce rate climbs sharply past 3s. Target LCP < 2.5s. The hero image or web font is usually the culprit.
P1 · Compress and right-size images; lazy-load below the foldImages are the majority of most page weight. Serve WebP/AVIF, exact display dimensions, loading="lazy" off-screen. Often the single biggest speed win.
P2 · Defer non-critical JavaScriptRender-blocking scripts in <head> delay everything. defer/async, and cut third-party tags you don't need.
P2 · TTFB under ~800msSlow server response points at no caching or no CDN. Put a CDN in front and cache HTML where you can.
P2 · Reserve space for images/embeds (no layout shift)Set width/height so content doesn't jump as it loads (CLS). Jumpy pages feel broken and cost conversions.
P3 · Keep total requests reasonableBundle assets; every extra third-party script is latency and a privacy liability.

IV. Persuasion — conversion people

P1 · One clear, specific value proposition above the foldIn five seconds a stranger must know what this is, who it's for, and why it's better. Vague hero copy is the most common silent killer.
P1 · One primary call-to-action, visually dominant, repeatedA page with no obvious next step converts near zero. One primary action; repeat it after each section.
P2 · Social proof near the CTATestimonials, logos, counts, ratings — placed exactly where the visitor hesitates, to absorb the risk of saying yes.
P2 · Benefits before features; scannableLead with the outcome, then the mechanism. Short paragraphs, bullets, subheads — people skim before they read.
P2 · Remove friction from the askEvery extra form field and every "create an account" step sheds conversions. Ask for the minimum.
P3 · Handle the top objection explicitlyPrice, trust, effort, or lock-in — name the visitor's #1 fear on the page and answer it.

V. Trust & reliability

P1 · Zero JavaScript console errors on loadErrors mean something is broken — often the analytics or the checkout. Open devtools; the console must be clean.
P2 · Valid HTTPS, no mixed contentA padlock warning at the moment of payment is fatal. No http:// assets on an https:// page.
P2 · Open Graph + Twitter card tagsWhen someone shares your link, it should render a title, description, and image — a large, free click-through lift on every share.
P2 · Real mobile layout, tap targets ≥ 44pxMost traffic is mobile. Test at 375px wide: nothing overflows, buttons are thumb-sized, text is legible without zoom.
P3 · A visible way to contact a humanAn email or form signals a real business behind the page and catches the objections you didn't anticipate.
P3 · A favicon and a proper 404Small polish signals that raise perceived legitimacy — and legitimacy is conversion.

How to run it

Top to bottom, once, writing down every miss. Fix all P1s first — they are where money and rankings actually move — then P2s. Re-run after each batch of fixes and confirm the specific thing you changed actually changed. Then put a reminder to re-audit in 90 days, because sites rot: a redeploy silently drops a meta tag, a new script adds a second of load, a design refresh buries the CTA. An audit is not a one-time event; it is a habit, and this is the checklist for the habit.
